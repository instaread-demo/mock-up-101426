Good Elf! Lyons was the best-reviewed show of the Fringe

Olaf Falafel No2 as Pierre Novellie tops an alternative analysis

Instaread

Elf Lyons had the best-reviewed comedy show of the 2026 Edinburgh Fringe, new analysis has shown.

Her offering, The Woman On The Edge, was also the show seen by the most critics, with 37 reviews averaging 4.63 stars.

The statistics, compiled by the British Comedy Guide, give the top ten best-reviewed shows as 

  1. Elf Lyons is The Woman on the Edge
  2. Olaf Falafel Presents: I Used to Work in a Helium Balloon Factory Until They Let Me Go (the best-reviewed free show)
  3. Chad Goes Deep - Chill Towards Enlightenment
  4. Garry Starr: Classic Penguins (returning for a third year)
  5. Lawrence Dodd: This Can't Be It
  6. Mark Thomas: 40 in Stand-Up Years
  7. Man Sings the Same Song Over and Over Again for an Hour
  8. Sammy J: Hero Complex
  9. Casey Filips: What a Character
  10. Milo Edwards: Catamaran

The site also published a separate list, compiled by comedian Sam See, which adjusts each review based on how it scores compared to the average star ratings given out by each critic. 

By that metric, the top ten is

  1. Pierre Novellie: Okay, One More Then Bedtime
  2. John Kearns: Tilting at Windmills
  3. Elf Lyons is The Woman on the Edge
  4. Hudson Hughes: At Your Service
  5. Brendan Scannell: The Abyss
  6. Sam Eley is Basil Crumbwick: Soul Sewage
  7. Chad Goes Deep - Chill Towards Enlightenment
  8. Mark Thomas: 40 in Stand-Up Years
  9. Garry Starr: Classic Penguins
  10. Scout Boxall: God's Favourite

In total, the British Comedy Guide recorded more than 6,600 comedy reviews this year – up 50 per cent on 2025. However, the number of shows increased by 41 per cent, largely due to many comedians performing shorter runs rather than the full month.

The most prolific reviewer was Jay Richardson, who works across a number of titles, including Chortle, with 118, ahead of this website’s editor, Steve Bennett, who wrote 110 of the 212 reviews we published during the festival.

The average scores were calculated using the Bayesian method – which essentially assumed each show got six three-star reviews on top of whatever they actually got, a technique designed to level the playing field when the actual number of reviews is small, so, for example, a show with a single 5-star review is not going to top the chart.
