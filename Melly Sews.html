Maxi Dress Makeover

How I took a summer maxi dress I no longer wear and sewed it into a dress I will wear more often.

Hey y’all, today I’m going to show you how I remade a maxi dress that I no longer wear into something I think I will wear more often in this phase of my life. This is another segment in my Why I Don’t Wear series of posts. This is the first time I’m taking something I originally sewed and don’t wear to remake instead of a thrifted item. So what happened?

I sewed this dress in 2018, eight years ago. You can see the original post for my maxi dress here. And my body is not exactly the same now as it was then.

One of my complaints now is that the dress is now just too tight across my chest and bum. That’s in addition to the fact that I no longer want to wear strapless bras if I can avoid them. You can see the beginnings of the bum problem in the original dress in the picture below, but as my hip measurement has increased since then, the issue got even bigger.

Luckily, because I sewed the dress myself, I also had some extra matching fabric. Point scored for hoarding fabric scraps! Check out a video of my problems with the dress now and what the makeover looks like below or on YouTube here.

As you can see in the video above, I basically just used the dress as fabric for an entirely new design. At this point my requirements were easy to wear and with pockets. So the design I finalized is a princess seamed bodice with a gentle V neck, cap sleeves with a very slight flutter for easy wear with a sweater or jacket, a gathered midi length A line skirt salvaged from the original, and side seam pockets.

Since I can wear a regular (ie, not strapless) bra with this dress and since it fits my bust and hips properly now, the odds of me wearing it are already 100% higher than me wearing the original dress now.

Before I settled on this sleeve, I tried a sleeve with a lot more flutter. You can see what that looked like below. I just placed the sleeve on my dress form without sewing it in place.

Though I love the way this sleeve looked on its own, I was concerned about it getting bunched up and wrinkled under a jacket or sweater. So I played around with some different muslin sleeves. The pattern piece that I ended up with is shown below.

I added an invisible zipper to the back of the dress for ease of getting into/out of it. You can see how to install an invisible zipper in this post.

And my favorite part of the new dress is the finish inside. I lined the bodice and used bias tape (actually, it was the straps from the original dress) to finish the armscyes cleanly.

The bias tape was sewn to the lining with an invisible stitch so you can’t see it on the outside at all. The hand sewing was worth it to me for this clean finish.

So that’s how I did a makeover for my maxi dress!
